# Stakater AI Usage Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how artificial intelligence (AI) tools are used within Stakater. The goal is to enable employees to safely leverage AI to improve productivity, software quality, and innovation, while protecting company data, customer trust, and compliance obligations. AI is treated as an augmentation tool, not a replacement for professional judgment, and all outputs remain the responsibility of the employee using them.

The intent of this policy is not to restrict usage, but to provide clear guardrails so employees can confidently adopt AI in their daily workflows. As AI becomes increasingly integrated into engineering and platform work, a shared understanding of acceptable usage reduces ambiguity and prevents inconsistent practices across teams.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and partners working with or on behalf of Stakater. It governs the use of all AI tools, including generative AI systems, coding assistants, chatbots, and AI-powered features embedded within IDEs, CI/CD systems, SaaS platforms, or internal tools.

The policy applies regardless of how AI is accessed, including web interfaces, APIs, plugins, or integrations, and covers both direct and indirect use of AI in daily work.

## 3. Approved AI Tools and Access

Anthropic’s Claude is the primary and approved AI tool for internal use at Stakater, particularly for software development and engineering workflows. All employees are expected to use the company-provided Claude account for work-related tasks rather than personal or unapproved AI tools.

Access to Claude must be provisioned through internal processes. Employees should contact Callum for account creation, access issues, or upgrades. This ensures proper onboarding, usage visibility, and alignment with company controls.

The use of alternative AI tools (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, or others) for company work is discouraged unless explicitly approved by leadership. This centralized approach reduces the risk of data leakage, ensures consistent governance, and prevents the emergence of “shadow AI,” where tools are used without oversight or security validation.

## 4. Acceptable Use

Employees are encouraged to use AI tools to enhance productivity, accelerate development workflows, and improve the quality of outputs. Acceptable use includes generating and refactoring code, writing and improving documentation, summarizing technical materials, assisting with debugging, creating test cases, and supporting design or architectural thinking.

AI should be treated as a collaborative assistant rather than an autonomous system. Employees must critically evaluate all outputs, verify correctness, and ensure alignment with project requirements and internal standards before use. AI-generated content should be considered a starting point, not a final deliverable.

In engineering contexts, AI is particularly useful for repetitive tasks, scaffolding, and exploration, but final implementation decisions and responsibility always remain with the developer.

## 5. Prohibited Use

AI tools must not be used in ways that expose Stakater to legal, security, compliance, or reputational risks. Employees must not input sensitive, confidential, or regulated data into AI systems unless explicitly approved and handled within secure, enterprise-controlled environments.

This includes, but is not limited to, customer data, credentials, secrets, API keys, private repositories, internal architecture details, financial information, and personally identifiable information. Even partial or indirect exposure of such data can create risk, as AI systems may process or retain submitted inputs.

Employees must also not use AI to generate misleading, deceptive, unethical, discriminatory, or harmful content, nor use it to bypass security controls, licensing restrictions, or established processes. Any attempt to use AI in ways that circumvent company policies is strictly prohibited.

## 6. Data Classification and Handling

All data shared with AI tools must comply with Stakater’s data classification and handling standards. As a general rule, only public, non-sensitive, or properly anonymized data may be used with AI tools unless explicit approval has been granted.

Employees should design prompts to minimize exposure of proprietary or identifiable information. Wherever possible, use generalized descriptions, pseudocode, or sanitized examples instead of real data or production configurations.

If a use case requires interaction with sensitive data, employees must consult with leadership or security stakeholders before proceeding. When in doubt, the safer assumption is that the data should not be shared.

## 7. Code and Intellectual Property

AI-generated code must be treated with the same rigor and scrutiny as human-written code. Employees are responsible for ensuring that generated code is secure, maintainable, properly tested, and aligned with internal engineering standards.

There should be no assumption that AI-generated code is free from intellectual property constraints. Outputs may resemble existing licensed or copyrighted material, and it is the responsibility of the employee to verify compliance with licensing requirements before committing code.

Additionally, AI-generated suggestions must be reviewed for hidden risks such as insecure patterns, outdated dependencies, or non-compliant implementations.

## 8. Security and Compliance

AI usage must align with all existing security policies, including secure development practices, access control requirements, and applicable regulations such as GDPR.

Employees must not use AI tools in ways that bypass established security processes, introduce unverified dependencies, or weaken system integrity. AI-generated configurations, scripts, or infrastructure changes must be reviewed carefully before implementation.

Any suspected security risks, unusual behavior, or potential data exposure incidents related to AI usage must be reported immediately to the appropriate internal teams.

## 9. Transparency and Accountability

Employees remain fully accountable for all work produced using AI tools. The use of AI does not transfer responsibility to the tool or its provider.

AI-generated content must be reviewed, validated, and, where appropriate, acknowledged as AI-assisted. Transparency is particularly important in collaborative and customer-facing contexts, where quality, accuracy, and trust are critical.

Clear accountability ensures that decisions remain explainable and that ownership is always maintained by the individual or team responsible for the work.

## 10. Enforcement

Failure to comply with this policy may result in disciplinary action, including revocation of AI tool access or further consequences depending on severity.

Stakater reserves the right to monitor usage of company-provided AI tools to ensure compliance with this policy, protect company assets, and maintain a secure working environment. Enforcement is intended to support responsible usage and protect both the company and its employees.
